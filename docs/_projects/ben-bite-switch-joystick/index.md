Electronic devices are essential to daily life today. This project allows users to easily interact with them, improving independence and quality of life. It enables Ben, an individual with cerebral palsy, to produce content by himself, supporting himself and the community he advocates for.
- A standard mouse is replaced with a joystick that allows the user to move their cursor and two bite switches for the left and right click.
- This wireless device easily mounts onto the user's wheelchair/desk and requires no additional software.
- This solution can be used by individuals that can't easily use a standard computer mouse, whether due to neurological disorders that affect mobility, like cerebral palsy, or hand/finger impairments.
- The joystick enables users with limited range of motion or grip strength in their hands to have precise control of the cursor.
- The bite switches provide a hands-free alternative to clicking or tapping, which often require greater finger strength/mobility.

See our documentation and assembly guide and check our our project video by clicking on the links in the left sidebar.

## My Contributions

- Led initial development of the bite switch during the hackathon; continued development with a 3-person team through final delivery
- Delivered and deployed the final assistive input system to the user; has been in regular use for 3+ months with no issues
reported across multiple follow-ups
- Collaborated directly with the user to gather requirements, test prototypes, and refine device placement, controls, and
usability
- Programmed two ESP32s and a Raspberry Pi Pico 2 W to form a distributed wireless control system connecting a
wheelchair-mounted joystick and bite switch to the user’s computer
- Implemented  simultaneous  BLE  communication  and  USB  HID  mouse  functionality  on  the  Pico  2  W  by  combining
functionality not available in existing example implementations
- Contributed to CAD, fabrication, electronics, and system integration and documented the final design for open-source
replication through TOM Global
